Our mission

Increase highway safety and reduce traffic friction.

Mixing high-speed drivers with standard commuters creates the most dangerous situations on the road. HyperLane gives each group its own space.

Speed gaps cause crashes

Fast drivers weaving through slower traffic create sudden braking, lane changes and collisions for everyone around them.

Not enough lanes

Drivers told us lane count is the biggest issue: too many cars for not enough road, and slow vehicles blocking every lane.

Time is the goal

Most highway drivers just want to get where they're going. Nearly every participant said their priority is reaching the destination.

The HyperLane solution

A separated, paid express lane, built for speed.

Like E-ZPass, but for velocity. Subscribers pay to access a barrier-separated lane with a higher sanctioned speed range. High-speed traffic is isolated from standard commuters, the city earns new revenue, and accident risk goes down in the general lanes.

  • Isolates high-speed traffic from standard commuters
  • Creates a new source of municipal revenue
  • Reduces accident risk across general lanes
  • Takes pressure off congested standard lanes
How it works

From sign-up to open road in four steps.

  1. 01

    Qualify

    Link your vehicle and driving record. We confirm you have no severe traffic infractions and build your Safety Rating.

  2. 02

    Subscribe

    Choose pay-per-mile, a trip pass or a monthly membership. Your status lives in the HyperLane app.

  3. 03

    Enter the lane

    Pull through a HyperLane gate. GPS identifies your vehicle and starts metering automatically, with no stopping and no tickets.

  4. 04

    Keep pace

    Stay above the adaptive velocity floor. If you drop below it, the app tells you when to exit so the lane keeps flowing.

A committed 10–20% segment

Participants expect most commuters to stay in toll-free lanes, but long-distance travelers, express commuters and drivers on tight schedules would pay for guaranteed time savings.

Takeaway: target long-distance and time-pressed drivers first.
“1 in 10 commuters and people running late.”
“If I pay, I save time. If I don't, the regular lanes might be less crowded.”
“Same concept as fast lanes where you can pay to skip the traffic.”

Pennies per mile, a few dollars per trip

Answers ranged from $0.10 per mile ($2 for a 20-mile stretch) to $3–5 per trip or $5 per month. One participant would not pay at all.

Takeaway: offer per-mile, per-trip and membership options.

Speed limits too low, not enough lanes

Drivers feel posted limits are low for how modern highways and vehicles are built, and that slower vehicles block faster traffic in every lane. The problem gets worse in populated areas.

Takeaway: separating speeds adds capacity where it's needed most.
“The speed limits feel too low for how the highways are built.”
“Lane count is the biggest issue. Too many cars for not enough road.”

At least 10 mph over the posted limit

Expected lane speeds ranged from a 70 mph minimum to around 100 mph. One participant recommended a strict minimum speed so slower drivers can't cause bottlenecks.

Takeaway: this led directly to Velocity Floors.

Congestion and cost

Drivers would skip the lane if it got congested enough to slow to normal speeds, or if per-mile charges added up faster than the time saved.

Takeaway: manage lane density, keep pricing clear and offer flat-rate options.
“Heavy traffic in the fast lane, or if not in a hurry and don't want to pay.”
“The price adds up far too fast to justify what time is saved.”

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Can't find what you're looking for? Get in touch.

Is HyperLane legal?

HyperLane runs as a municipally sanctioned lane with its own speed range, the way HOV and express toll lanes work today. Drivers inside the lane follow HyperLane's posted range instead of the general lanes' limit.

How is my trip billed?

The HyperLane app uses GPS to detect when you enter and exit the lane, then bills your account by the mile or against your active pass, similar to E-ZPass.

What happens if I drive too slowly?

Each lane segment has an adaptive velocity floor. If you fall under it, the app sends an alert and guides you to the next exit so traffic keeps moving.

Who can qualify?

Any licensed driver with a verified record free of severe traffic infractions and an up-to-date vehicle maintenance log. Your Safety Rating determines ongoing access.

What if the HyperLane gets congested?

We monitor lane density in real time and limit new entries when traffic builds, so the lane stays meaningfully faster than the general lanes.

How does this help drivers who don't use it?

Moving high-speed traffic out of the general lanes reduces weaving and congestion, which our interviewees expected would shorten normal commute times too.
